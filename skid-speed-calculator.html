Method & limits

The calculator solves the work–energy relation v = √(2 · f · g · d), folded into the field constants used in reconstruction practice: S = √(254 · d · f · η) for metres to km/h, and S = √(30 · d · f · η) for feet to mph. Grade is applied as f_eff = f + G/100, so an uphill skid stops shorter and an equal mark implies more speed.

Treat every result as a minimum. It accounts only for energy consumed by the visible friction mark — not pre-skid deceleration before the tyres locked, energy carried into an impact, post-impact travel, ABS cycling that leaves faint or intermittent marks, or a rolling start to the mark. Drag factor should come from a skid test on the same surface where one is available; the presets are typical published ranges, not measurements.
